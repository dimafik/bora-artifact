\subsection{Throughput under attack and the corrected saturation point}
\label{sec:caliper}

\begin{figure*}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/fig_eval_corrected.pdf}

cross-checked against ledger block-height delta).
\textbf{(a)} Committed throughput versus offered load for clean,

\textbf{(b)} Adversarial leadership acquisition over forced

An audit of the original saturation sweeps (offered $600$--$900$
tx/s) uncovered a client-side measurement artifact: that offered
load exceeds the single-host cluster's committed-throughput ceiling
of $\approx 570$ tx/s, so the orderer queue grew without bound and
Caliper's gateway commit-status calls returned
\texttt{DEADLINE\_EXCEEDED}. Every transaction was nonetheless
committed on-ledger --- block-height growth confirms a sustained
$\approx 570$ tx/s commit rate --- but the client never confirmed
the commits, so the per-round figures reflected the offered
\emph{send rate}, not confirmed committed throughput. We therefore
re-ran the entire campaign at sub-ceiling rates ($100$--$500$
tx/s), where commits confirm within the gateway deadline, and
cross-checked every run against the ledger block-height delta.

Figure~\ref{fig:eval}(a) reports the corrected throughput. The
$+500$\,ms single-follower attack lowers committed throughput by
$15$--$25\%$ and raises mean latency from $\approx 50$\,ms to
$\approx 2$\,s. \emph{BORA does not recover this throughput}: with
\texttt{orderer3} blacklisted it tracks the attack curve to within
$\pm 3\%$ at every rate and across seeds. This is the correct,
expected behaviour of the protocol. The bounded blacklist governs
only \emph{leader eligibility} (Algorithm~\ref{alg:hook}); it does
not change the leader's replication to, or quorum wait on, a slow
follower. A network-delayed follower is absorbed by Raft's existing
$3/5$ quorum margin whether or not BORA is active, so BORA is
correctly neutral on follower-delay throughput. An earlier
draft of this paper reported ``$+10.6\%$/$+14.5\%$ recovery''
figures in this regime; those were the send-rate artifact above and
do not survive a confirmed-commit measurement, so we retract them.

\paragraph*{Where BORA's value is measurable.}
BORA's purpose is leadership integrity, so its value is visible in
\emph{who holds the leadership}, not in single-follower throughput.
Figure~\ref{fig:eval}(b) measures this directly. Over repeated
forced elections (twelve elections per campaign), the most
frequently elected orderer won leadership $33\%$ of the time
without BORA; when BORA blacklists that node its win rate falls to
$\approx 14\%$ --- a $\approx 2.4\times$ reduction --- while every
election still produced a valid leader, so liveness is preserved.
This is exactly BORA's security objective: it denies a targeted
adversary the leadership it would need to bias block ordering,
without the hard demotion that would break the simulation relation
of Theorem~\ref{thm:safety}. The residual non-zero win rate is the
designed consequence of the fail-open, advisory mechanism --- BORA
never forces a yield it cannot confirm, so a blacklisted node
occasionally campaigns in the brief window before fresh advice is
read.

\paragraph*{Safety.}
Across every condition measured --- clean, single- and
multi-follower delay at every rate, and the leadership-exclusion
campaign --- the cluster committed every transaction with zero
safety violations, the only universal guarantee and the
trace-refinement content of Theorem~\ref{thm:safety}.
